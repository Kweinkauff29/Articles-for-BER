Is Southwest Florida’s Housing Market Heating Up? What FGCU’s November 2025 Data Really Reveals for Buyers & Sellers

Author: Bonita Springs-Estero REALTORS® • Published 2025-11-04

The latest FGCU Regional Economic Indicators report shows a mixed picture for Southwest Florida. Tourism and travel are up as winter approaches, but retail sales have softened. The region’s three airports handled about 1.5 million passengers in September 2025, roughly 5% more than a year ago, and tourist bed tax revenues (Jan–Aug) reached $66.2 million (up 5.8% YoY). By contrast, local taxable sales were $2.920 billion in July 2025, down 8.1% from July 2024. In other words, visitors are back, but residents are spending cautiously.

Jobs and Retail Trends

The labor market remains tight but cooling. FGCU reports Southwest Florida’s unemployment rate at 4.3% in August 2025, up from 3.6% a year earlier. Job growth has slowed, and leisure/hospitality sector jobs have dipped year-over-year. For consumers, this softening translates to slower retail activity. The region’s taxable sales drop mirrors national reports of sluggish retail, even as inflation and interest rates remain concerns.

Housing Supply & Demand

In the housing sector, supply is finally growing. Single-family building permits in August were 1,397, about 11% lower than a year earlier, reflecting cautious new construction. However, active listings have climbed. As of September 2025, Lee County had 10,670 homes for sale (14% more than Sept 2024), Collier 5,291 (+13%), and Charlotte 3,458 (+6%). This rise – roughly a 10–12% regional gain in listings – gives buyers more choices than in the tight market of 2021–22.

Meanwhile, demand remains solid. In September 2025 Southwest Florida coastal counties saw 1,903 closed single-family home sales, up 18.6% from a year ago. Florida Realtors® reports statewide sales also rising (Florida single-family sales +13.6% YoY in Sept), driven largely by lower mortgage rates. Nationally, NAR noted existing-home sales rose in September as falling rates “lift home sales” and improve affordability. Locally, median prices are easing: in Sept 2025 the Cape Coral–Fort Myers median was $375,000 (down 2.1% YoY), Naples $772,500 (down 1.0%), Punta Gorda $330,000 (down 1.6%). In sum, sales are up as some late buyers return, but prices are leveling off.



Consumer confidence is softening. FGCU highlights that Florida’s Consumer Sentiment Index fell about 1.5 points in September 2025. A Florida Realtors® news brief similarly reports U.S. sentiment down 1.5 points in October, with inflation still consumers’ top concernIn practical terms, buyers are more cautious. Yet the Federal Reserve’s recent quarter-point rate cut (to 3.75–4.00%) has improved mortgage affordability. Economists point out that lower rates tend to “lift home sales” by enabling more buyers to qualify.

Implications for REALTORS® and Homeowners

What do these trends mean locally? For sellers, early season is still a good time to list. More visitors and buyers are active now, and new listings are rising ahead of the full-season influx. However, listings growth means more competition. Sellers may need to price competitively or make strategic concessions (recent data show thousands of recent price cuts as the market recalibrates). Homeowners considering listing should act soon, before January’s inventory jump.

For buyers, the market offers opportunities. Rising inventory and builder incentives (many homebuilders are offering year-end deals) mean buyers have more bargaining power. Mortgage rates are somewhat lower than mid-year, so locking in a favorable rate now could be wise. That said, affordability pressures (insurance and taxes) remain a challenge, so consumers should budget carefully.

In short, the FGCU data show a classic seasonal shift: demand is solid but more value-driven and supply is picking up. Buyers should be selective and ready to act; sellers should prepare for more competition.
