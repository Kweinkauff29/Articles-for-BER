A Nearly Flat Summer for Florida Tourism. What the Numbers Say and What They Do Not.

VISIT FLORIDA's preliminary estimates show almost no change in total visitors from July through September. Some pieces rose, some slipped, and none of them tell us on their own what will happen in the housing market.

Published November 24, 2026 • By Coconut Coast Organization of REALTORS®

Florida's visitor total barely moved this summer, according to VISIT FLORIDA. Its November 16 release estimates 34.73 million visitors in the third quarter of 2026, a change of 0.1%.

That is a steady number. It is also a statewide number, and it is preliminary. For anyone buying, selling or working in Southwest Florida real estate, the useful questions are what sits underneath it, how much of it applies here, and what it can fairly be used to say.

Reading the percentages: The changes shown are the ones VISIT FLORIDA reported. They compare the third quarter of 2026 with Q3 2025. The estimates are preliminary and may be revised.

What VISIT FLORIDA Reported

Most of the quarter's visitors came from within the United States. Domestic travelers made up 31.86 million of the 34.73 million total. By our arithmetic, that is about 92 of every 100 visitors. The domestic estimate rose slightly, while the overseas and Canadian estimates each declined.

July was stronger than the quarter as a whole. VISIT FLORIDA reported a 1.8% increase for that month, compared with 0.1% for the full three months.

About those No. 1 rankings

The release also includes No. 1 market share rankings: 16.0% of domestic visitors, 28.4% of overseas visitors and 21.5% of Canadian visitors. Those shares are for the first half of 2026, not the third quarter. If you quote them, keep that time period attached. (VISIT FLORIDA)

Three Cautions Before You Use the Numbers

They are statewide

Florida is many markets. A statewide total can hold steady while one coast rises and another falls. These figures do not tell us how Lee or Collier County did.

They measure different things

Visitors, people boarding planes and hotel rooms sold are separate counts. Residents also board flights, and visitors also drive. One line moving does not explain another.

Visitors are not buyers

A visit may be a family vacation, a work trip or a first look at a neighborhood. A visitor count cannot tell us which, and it does not measure homes sold.

What We Can Say About Southwest Florida

We are keeping this section short on purpose. This article does not include a local visitor count for the third quarter, so we are not going to guess at one.

The local tourism figures below come from CCOR's earlier reporting on the Regional Economic Indicators published by Florida Gulf Coast University. They cover late winter and spring 2026. They are useful background, but they are older than the statewide release and should not be read as third-quarter results.

  • Airport passengers, March 2026: 1,529,020 across the region's three airports, seasonally adjusted, up 1.8% from a year earlier. (CCOR summary of FGCU's May 2026 report)
  • Airport passengers, April 2026: 1,489,390, down 2.3% from a year earlier. (CCOR May 2026 market report)
  • Tourist tax revenues, February 2026: $9.0 million in the coastal counties, seasonally adjusted and adjusted for inflation, up 12.3% from February 2025. (CCOR summary of FGCU's May 2026 report)
  • Tourist tax revenues, March 2026: $8.6 million, up 8.9% from a year earlier. (CCOR May 2026 market report)

Even in those few months, the signals did not all point the same way. Passenger counts rose in March and fell in April while tourist tax revenues were higher in both months reported. That is a good reason not to lean on a single number.

Where Housing Fits, Carefully

CCOR's most recent monthly report covers October 2026, the month after the third quarter ended. Across the Southwest Florida MLS footprint, it showed 2,534 closed sales, down 4.6% from October 2025; 2,627 pending sales, down 2.0%; and 23,475 active listings, down 9.1%. (CCOR October 2026 market stats)

We put those numbers here so you have both in one place. They cover a different period and a different subject, and nothing in either report shows that tourism caused any of them. Home sales depend on many things a visitor count does not capture, including prices, financing, insurance costs and a household's own plans.

How to Put This to Use

For REALTORS®

  • Quote it accurately. Say statewide, say preliminary, and name the quarter.
  • Check your own data. Compare showing requests, open house sign-ins and website inquiries with the same weeks last year. Those describe your market better than a state total.
  • Plan for short trips. Ask out-of-area clients when they will be here, then line up showings, lender calls and documents before they arrive.

For Buyers

  • Use a visit well. See neighborhoods at different times of day and drive the routes you would use.
  • Look at full costs. Ask about insurance, flood zone, association fees and any planned assessments for each home.
  • Considering a rental? Ask for that property's actual rental history. Statewide hotel rates cannot predict it.

For Sellers

  • Price from comparable sales. A visitor total is not evidence of what your home will bring.
  • Make showings easy. Some buyers have only a few days in town. Talk with your agent about notice, access and a virtual tour.
  • Have answers ready. Gather roof, insurance, permit and association information before you list.

If a client is thinking about short-term rental income

The hotel figures describe hotels. They do not measure vacation rental bookings, and they do not describe any one condo or house. Before anyone counts on rental income, read the association's documents for lease limits, confirm city and county requirements, and talk with a tax professional about the taxes that apply.

Wording you can adapt

VISIT FLORIDA's preliminary estimate is that statewide visitors were nearly flat in the third quarter of 2026, at about 34.73 million. That is background, not a forecast for our market. For your decision, let's look at recent sales, current competition and showing activity for homes like yours.

The Bottom Line

Florida's summer was steady in total and mixed underneath. Domestic visits edged up, overseas and Canadian visits slipped, fewer people boarded flights at the airports counted, and hotels sold more rooms at higher average rates. It is reasonable to find that interesting. It is not enough, by itself, to change a price or a plan. Your local market data and your own goals should still lead the way.
