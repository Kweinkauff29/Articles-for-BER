Protect Your Property from Deed and Title Fraud

Free Property Fraud Alerts in Lee County and Across Florida

Quick takeaways for REALTORS® and property owners

Property fraud (often called deed fraud or title theft) typically starts when a bad actor records a fraudulent document in a county’s Official Records. That’s why early awareness matters—and why Florida’s Clerk & Comptroller offices offer free alert services to help property owners spot suspicious activity faster.

Here’s the headline: Lee Clerk’s Property Fraud Alert is free and can email notifications within 24 hours when certain land records—like a deed or mortgage—are recorded in Lee County using a registered name or parcel ID.
Watch: Property Fraud Alert explained in 60 seconds.

What Lee Clerk’s Property Fraud Alert does (and does not do)

Lee Clerk describes Property Fraud Alert as a free service designed to alert property owners of possible fraud by emailing notifications within 24 hours when a deed, mortgage, or other land record with the subscriber’s registered name or parcel ID is recorded into Lee County’s Official Records.

Lee Clerk also highlights an important limitation that every REALTOR® should repeat to clients:

  • It does not prevent fraud. It’s an early-warning tool so owners can respond more quickly if something suspicious appears in the public record.
  • It does not apply to court-filed documents. The alert system is tied to land documents recorded in Official Records, not filings made in court proceedings.

Why does this matter in real life? Lee Clerk notes that a recorded deed can even change the property owner’s mailing address in property appraiser and tax collector systems—meaning TRIM notices or tax bills may be sent to a different address, and unpaid taxes can eventually lead to a tax deed auction.

That “mailing address change” risk is one reason this is such a high-value, low-friction consumer protection tip to share with: absentee owners, snowbirds, landlords, investors, heirs, owners of vacant land, and anyone holding title in an LLC or trust.

How to sign up in Lee County (and how to set it up correctly)

Lee Clerk’s program allows you to register personal names, business names, trusts, and individual parcels, and you can repeat the registration process to add as many names/parcels as you need.

A practical, REALTOR®-friendly onboarding checklist you can share with clients:

  • Register the owner name(s) as they appear in public records (and consider multiple variations if the name could be indexed differently). Lee Clerk explicitly recommends reviewing their registration tips/FAQs because naming conventions can improve results.
  • If available for the county program, register the parcel ID as well. Lee Clerk notes alerts can be tied to parcel registrations.
  • Add business/trust names when applicable (for LLC-owned property or trust-held property). Lee Clerk confirms registering business names and trusts is supported.
  • If a client owns properties in multiple counties, they should sign up in each county where they own property (see statewide section below).

Florida-wide resources: free property alert services in all 67 counties

If your client owns property outside Lee County—or owns multiple Florida properties—this is the part worth bookmarking.

The Florida Bar reported that all 67 Clerks of the Circuit Court and Comptrollers statewide now offer free property alert services, and explained these programs alert subscribers when a deed, mortgage, or other official record is recorded in their name in a county’s Official Records.

That same Florida Bar report notes that alert types vary by county (email, SMS, or phone call), and it points residents to the Florida Clerks & Comptrollers statewide page that lists alert services by county.

Florida lawmakers also enacted HB 1419 (Real Property Fraud), which includes requirements for clerks to create and operate an opt-in recording notification service and includes other fraud-related provisions.

What the FBI says about deed/title schemes

The FBI has warned that quit claim deed fraud / home title theft involves criminals forging documents to record a phony transfer of ownership, then attempting to sell the property, take out a mortgage on it, or rent it out—forcing the rightful owner into a costly fight to reclaim title.

That pattern aligns with why county-level alert tools focus on recorded documents: they’re designed to give owners faster visibility into suspicious recordings.

Florida context: high overall IC3 complaint volume

The same IC3 report shows Florida ranked among the top states by total IC3 complaints (Florida listed with 52,191 complaints in the report’s overall state statistics table).

This is not “property fraud only,” but it supports a reasonable takeaway: Florida property owners are operating in a high-volume fraud environment, which makes low-effort monitoring tools worth sharing.

What to do if you receive an alert

A fraud alert does not automatically mean fraud—but it does mean, “a recorded document matched what you’re monitoring.” It’s the prompt to verify.

Lee Clerk’s FAQ notes that the alert includes instructions on how to view the recorded document and how to proceed if you feel the activity is fraudulent.

Most Florida clerk programs give similar guidance: review the document, and if suspicious, contact law enforcement and seek legal counsel. Miami-Dade’s materials specifically recommend notification of law enforcement and seeking attorney guidance if a recorded deed or other document is fraudulent.

A practical “client-safe” action list:

  1. Open the alert and view the recorded document in Official Records using the document number/link provided.
  2. If you recognize the transaction, document it and move on.
  3. If you do not recognize it, treat it as time-sensitive and contact:
    • Your local law enforcement agency (Sheriff/Police)
    • An attorney (title/real estate litigation as appropriate)
    • Your Property Appraiser (when ownership records are affected)
  4. Preserve evidence: save the email alert, document number, screenshots, and any related communications.

REALTOR® talking points: how to share this with clients without creating liability

This is the language tone that keeps your client informed and keeps you in a safe lane:

  • “This is a free monitoring tool offered by the Clerk’s office. It doesn’t prevent fraud, but it can alert you quickly if something gets recorded that matches your name or parcel.”
  • “If you get an alert, review the document right away. If it looks wrong, contact law enforcement and an attorney.”
  • “If you own property in more than one Florida county, sign up in each county—Florida Clerks notes these services are available statewide.”
