FinCEN Delays - New Real Estate Reporting Rule

Author: • Published 2025-10-28

FinCEN’s new rule, issued in Aug. 2024, was designed to capture cash deals that bypass the banking system. It requires title companies, closing attorneys and other settlement agents to report non-financed (all-cash) home sales when the buyer is a company or trust. Without the rule, such transactions are not scrutinized by bank monitors, creating a potential avenue for money laundering.

The September 2025 announcement included a formal exemptive relief order. Along with the postponement, FinCEN released the actual reporting form industry leaders had been waiting for, enabling firms to train their staff and update systems by the new deadline. The extra time was widely welcomed; many companies had warned that a Dec. 1 start left too little time to implement the requirements. Still, industry experts note that legal challenges continue: for example, a major title company has sued over the rule’s validity, and the American Land Title Association has filed to support that challenge. Real estate professionals should watch for any updates while proceeding with planning and training as if the rule will take effect in March 2026.

What is FinCEN?

The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) is a bureau of the U.S. Department of the Treasury. Its mission is to safeguard the financial system from illicit use, counter money laundering and the financing of terrorism, and promote national security. FinCEN does this by using legal authorities under the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) to collect, analyze, and share financial intelligence with law enforcement and national security partners.

What FinCEN actually does

  • Collects and analyzes BSA data (e.g., Suspicious Activity Reports, Currency Transaction Reports) and disseminates it to investigative agencies.
  • Issues and enforces anti-money-laundering rules for parts of the financial and non-financial sectors, including programs that require transparency about “beneficial owners” behind companies.
  • Runs the FinCEN Exchange, a public-private partnership that accelerates information-sharing on emerging threats.

Implications for REALTORS®

  • More preparation time. The rule’s pushback means brokers and agents can use the winter season to train staff and integrate the new report form into their closing processes instead of racing to meet a Dec. 1 deadline.
  • Know the rules. REALTORS® should familiarize themselves with the details: the requirement applies to all-cash residential transactions where the buyer is an entity (like an LLC, trust or corporation), not an individual. Staying ahead on these definitions will help agents advise clients correctly.
  • Monitor developments. Although delayed, the rule is still coming (for now) in March 2026. Industry groups and attorneys are watching court cases that could change or block the rule. Agents should follow updates from Florida Realtors, NAR and local title companies and continue preparing as if the rule will go into effect.

Implications for Homebuyers

  • Expect extra paperwork. Buyers who pay cash using a business entity or trust should be ready to provide additional information at closing. This includes things like the entity’s taxpayer ID and details about where the funds come from. The delay means you have time to gather these documents, but you will eventually need them.
  • Compliance beyond mortgages. This rule does not affect typical bank-financed purchases. It only applies to all-cash deals meeting the buyer-entity criteria. Even so, buyers should understand that cash closings to a trust or company will trigger federal reporting (with the new deadline in place).
  • Work with informed agents. Clients in Bonita Springs and Estero should rely on knowledgeable REALTORS® to guide them. Agents can explain whether a sale will need to be reported and help ensure all legal requirements are met. Staying in close contact with your agent about any LLC or trust arrangements will make compliance smoother.

Why REALTORS® should care

Real estate has long been a target for illicit finance. FinCEN’s Residential Real Estate Rule (RRE) is part of a broader effort to bring transparency to all-cash residential property transfers involving legal entities and trusts. FinCEN has postponed required reporting under the RRE rule until March 1, 2026, but the framework—and the expectation of compliance—remains in place.
