CCOR MARKET INTELLIGENCE • SEPTEMBER 2026

Inventory Tightens. Contracts Improve. Prices Diverge.

Southwest Florida's September 2026 real estate numbers show fewer available homes, improving contract activity and two different pricing stories for single-family homes and condominiums.

September 2026 was a study in contrasts for Southwest Florida real estate. Closed sales across the entire Southwest Florida MLS declined compared with a year earlier, but more properties went under contract, active inventory fell and homes that sold spent less time on the market.

The combination is important. A decline in completed transactions does not necessarily mean buyers have disappeared, just as a reduction in available inventory does not automatically signal rising prices. September's statistics show why buyers, sellers and REALTORS® need to look beyond any single headline.

The property-type breakdown is especially revealing. Across Lee, Collier and Hendry counties, the September median price for single-family homes increased 2.4%, while the condominium median declined 4.8%. Both property types saw stronger pending sales and lower inventory than in September 2025.

Understanding the reporting areas: The six headline statistics above cover the entire SWFLAMLS. The single-family and condominium comparisons below come from CCOR's September 2026 Local Market Update for Lee, Collier and Hendry counties. These are different reporting areas and their totals should not be combined.

Three Signals Explain September

01 / Less Inventory

Active listings declined 12.6% across the entire SWFLAMLS. Buyers faced fewer available options than a year earlier.

02 / More Contracts

Pending sales increased 2.5%, even as closed transactions fell 6.8%. Contracts and closings measure different stages of activity.

03 / Faster Turnover

Median days on market declined 24.3% to 56 days. Homes that sold generally moved through the market faster.

Perhaps the most encouraging indicator is the increase in pending sales. A pending transaction represents a property that has gone under contract but has not necessarily completed the closing process. Its movement can provide an early indication of demand, although not every contract closes.

Meanwhile, the 12.6% reduction in active inventory suggests the supply environment has become tighter than it was in September 2025. That can help well-positioned listings compete, but it does not eliminate the importance of pricing, property condition, financing costs or buyer expectations.

The market is not uniformly rising or falling. It is becoming more selective, with available inventory tightening and different property segments following different price paths.

Single-family pricing showed modest resilience, with the September median increasing from $420,000 to $430,000. Pending sales rose 5.3%, and inventory declined 12.7%.

Condominium buyers, meanwhile, completed 6.7% more transactions than in September 2025, but the median sale price declined from $346,500 to $330,000. The combination of stronger transaction activity and lower prices illustrates why sales growth does not necessarily translate into price growth.

Condominium purchasing decisions can also involve additional considerations, including association budgets, insurance expenses, reserve funding, special assessments and lender eligibility. These factors can create substantial differences between individual buildings and communities.

The year-to-date data shows why September's decline in entire-MLS closings should not be interpreted as evidence of a broad collapse in housing demand.

Over the first nine months of 2026, single-family transactions increased 7.9% while condominium closings increased 22.4% in the three-county report. Pending sales also increased year to date: 8.6% for single-family properties and 21.3% for condominiums.

Those gains do not guarantee further improvement in the fourth quarter, but they provide important context for interpreting one month's results.

What About Bonita Springs, Estero and Naples?

For buyers and sellers in Bonita Springs and Estero, regional market statistics are useful starting points, but they do not replace neighborhood-level analysis.

Waterfront properties, golf-community homes, newer construction, attached villas and older condominium buildings can each experience different inventory conditions and pricing pressures. Even homes located in the same ZIP code may face very different competitive environments.

A Separate Naples Benchmark

According to the Naples Area Board of REALTORS® September 2026 report, covering Collier County excluding Marco Island, the Naples market recorded 623 closed sales, up 2.5% compared with September 2025.

The median closed price increased 4.5% to $575,000, while active inventory declined 17.3% to 4,240 properties. Months of inventory decreased from 7.8 to 5.5.

Naples therefore illustrates an important point: markets throughout Southwest Florida may share a trend of declining inventory while experiencing different changes in sales volume and median prices.

The Naples reporting area and methodology differ from CCOR's regional statistics. These numbers are provided as a separate benchmark and should not be added to the SWFLAMLS totals.

How Does Florida Compare?

Statewide data from Florida Realtors® offers another useful comparison. Existing single-family home sales declined 3.5% in September 2026 compared with a year earlier. Condo-townhouse closed sales were nearly unchanged, declining 0.1%.

The statewide single-family median increased 1.2% to $415,000, while the condo-townhouse median declined 1.8% to $293,500. Months of supply measured 4.4 for single-family homes and 7.7 for condo-townhouse properties.

These statewide figures reinforce the importance of property-type differences, although statewide data cannot determine the value or marketing outlook for an individual Southwest Florida property.

What September Means for Buyers and Sellers

For Buyers

  • Compare available properties within the same community and property type.
  • Track recent sales, price reductions and days on market.
  • For condos, review association financials, assessments, insurance and financing requirements.

For Sellers

  • Price according to current, genuinely comparable sales.
  • Evaluate competing listings and property-specific ownership costs.
  • Remember that declining inventory does not automatically guarantee a higher sale price.

For REALTORS®

  • Distinguish pending sales from completed transactions.
  • Clearly identify the geographic area and property types behind every statistic.
  • Use neighborhood and building-level comparables to explain conditions beyond the regional median.

What to Watch Heading Into the Seasonal Market

As Southwest Florida approaches its traditional seasonal real estate period, several questions will help determine the direction of the market.

  • Will the improvement in pending sales translate into additional closings?
  • Will inventory continue tightening as seasonal listings enter the market?
  • Will single-family median prices remain resilient while condominium prices seek greater stability?
  • How will mortgage rates, insurance expenses and other ownership costs affect buyer decisions?

September's data suggests that market activity remains meaningful, but participants are responding differently depending on location, property type and price point.

For Southwest Florida buyers and sellers, the most important question is no longer simply whether the overall market is up or down. It is how the market for their particular property is performing.
